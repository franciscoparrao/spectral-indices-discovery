\section*{Cross-Domain Validation: Land Cover Classification}
\label{sec:s_landcover}

To test whether the SR feature engineering framework generalizes beyond hydrothermal alteration, we applied the same methodology to land cover classification, a fundamentally different remote sensing problem. Using ESA WorldCover 2021 labels \citep{Zanaga2022} and Sentinel-2 imagery from central Germany (Thuringia/Bavaria border, \ang{50.35} N, \ang{11.15} E), we sampled 6{,}612 pixels across five land cover classes: Forest, Grassland, Cropland, Built-up, and Water. PySR discovered five class-specific indices (complexity $\leq 8$) using a lighter search configuration than the alteration experiments (\texttt{niterations} = 80, \texttt{populations} = 15, \texttt{select\_k\_features} = 4), commensurate with the smaller dataset.

Table~\ref{tab:s_landcover} shows the feature engineering results, which replicate the key pattern observed in alteration mapping.

\begin{table*}[ht]
\centering

\label{tab:s_landcover}
\begin{tabular}{llcccccccc}
\toprule
Classifier & Features & $n$ feat. & BA & mAUC & Forest & Grass & Crop & Built & Water \\
\midrule

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table*}

The combined SR + classical feature set achieves the highest mean AUC (0.926) for RF, exceeding both raw bands (0.920) and SR indices alone (0.902). The AUC gap between SR-only and raw bands ($\Delta = -0.018$) is comparable to the leakage-free discovery cost measured for the alteration domain under nested cross-validation ($-0.011$ stratified, $-0.018$ polygon-disjoint; main text Table~6), despite the lighter search configuration used here (five features, \texttt{select\_k\_features} = 4 vs.\ 5 for alteration). The central finding, that combining SR-discovered features with classical indices improves over raw bands, replicates in a completely different application domain, supporting the generality of the framework.

The SR-discovered formulas for land cover are listed in Table~\ref{tab:s_lc_formulas}. The NIR band (B08) appears in four of five formulas, reflecting the dominance of vegetation-sensitive spectral regions in land cover discrimination. However, unlike the alteration indices where visible and SWIR bands dominate, the land cover formulas also incorporate SWIR bands (B11 in Grassland and Cropland, B12 in Built-up) to distinguish spectrally similar vegetated classes and impervious surfaces.

\begin{table}[ht]
\centering
\caption{SR-discovered indices for land cover classes. Variables $x_0$--$x_9$ in PySR correspond to bands in alphabetical order: B02, B03, B04, B05, B06, B07, B08, B11, B12, B8A. Formulas shown with band names.}
\label{tab:s_lc_formulas}
\begin{tabular}{llcc}
\toprule
Class & Formula & Compl. & AUC \\
\midrule
Forest   & $0.021 / [(\text{B08} - 0.30)^2 + \text{B03}]$ & 8 & 0.73 \\
Grassland & $\text{B08} \cdot (\text{B11}/(\text{B04} + 0.14))^2$ & 8 & 0.77 \\
Cropland  & $\text{B8A} \cdot \text{B08} \cdot (0.21/\text{B11})^2$ & 8 & 0.73 \\
Built-up  & $12.8 \cdot \text{B12} \cdot (\text{B8A} - 0.71)^2$ & 8 & 0.81 \\
Water     & $[(\log(\text{B08} + 0.33))^2 + \text{B05}]^2$ & 8 & 0.82 \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{table}
