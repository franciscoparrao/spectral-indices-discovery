TITLE
============================================================
Symbolic Regression as Spectral Feature Engineering: Discovering Interpretable Indices for Hydrothermal Alteration Mapping from Sentinel-2

============================================================
ABSTRACT (plain text, ~425 words — current version from main.tex)
============================================================
The central contribution of this work is not a single spectral index, but a transferable methodology for automated spectral feature discovery. We present symbolic regression (SR) as a general-purpose framework for automated and interpretable spectral feature engineering in multispectral classification: the framework requires only labeled pixels and a sensor's band configuration as input, and can be re-applied at any new site to discover locally optimal formulas. The key empirical result supporting this claim is that a compact set of SR-derived features preserves nearly all the discriminative information contained in the original multispectral bands. We demonstrate the approach on hydrothermal alteration mapping with Sentinel-2 — a challenging test case with six spectrally similar classes — using field-mapped ground truth from Chile's Atacama Region for training and two geologically independent sites for validation: Chile's Coquimbo Region (200 km distant, same metallogenic belt) and Cuprite, Nevada, USA (different continent and geological province). PySR discovered six class-specific indices (1–3 bands, complexity 3–8 expression tree nodes) on a 70% training partition, evaluated on a locked 30% hold-out subset. A Random Forest trained on six SR-derived features (mean AUC = 0.898) matches its performance on all 10 raw Sentinel-2 bands (0.899), formally demonstrating near-lossless dimensionality reduction (TOST p < 0.001, ε = 0.01). Under polygon-disjoint spatial cross-validation, which removes autocorrelation between adjacent pixels, the equivalence is preserved (SR 0.815 vs. raw 0.822; Δ = -0.007), confirming that the result is not an artefact of spatial autocorrelation. SR beats mutual-information band selection by +0.02 to +0.06 AUC, while PCA(6) reaches higher absolute AUC (+0.04 on average); SR and PCA therefore occupy distinct points on the accuracy–interpretability trade-off, with SR providing closed-form, sensor-agnostic, physically interpretable features at near-equivalent accuracy to the raw bands. This feature engineering equivalence replicates at Cuprite (SR 0.942 vs. raw 0.940), confirming cross-continent generalization of the methodology rather than of any single formula. Combining SR and classical indices yields the best performance (mean AUC = 0.920), confirmed across four classifiers and two deep learning architectures. As standalone indices, the propylitic formula (B03 − 0.48·B11; AUC = 0.91, DeLong p < 0.001) significantly outperforms classical indices, though standalone transferability varies by class — a property expected of site-fitted formulas and distinct from the transferability of the discovery framework itself. Physical interpretation confirms that discovered formulas exploit mineral absorption mechanisms consistent with spectroscopic theory. A cross-domain proof-of-concept on land cover classification replicates the key pattern (SR + classical AUC 0.926 vs. raw 0.920), supporting that the framework generalizes beyond geological applications. The methodology is sensor-agnostic, reproducible, and applicable to a broad range of multispectral classification problems where labeled pixels are available and compact, interpretable features are preferred over black-box models.
